What Happened to Centriq?

Centriq was the leading home maintenance app for years. It was featured by This Old House, had strong App Store ratings, and was used in over 400,000 homes. Homeowners used it to store manuals, track service schedules, and photograph model plates and warranty information.

Then, in January 2025, Centriq abruptly shut down. The company posted a brief notice, and within weeks, all user data was permanently deleted from their servers. No export tool was provided. No transition period. Homeowners who had spent years building a digital record of their home's maintenance history lost everything overnight.

The shutdown was a painful reminder of a real risk with cloud-only apps: when the company goes away, your data goes with it. Several of the alternatives below have learned from Centriq's failure and offer local storage or export features to protect against this exact scenario.

What to Look for in a Replacement

Before jumping into the list, here's what matters most when choosing a Centriq replacement:

  • Data portability. Can you export your data? If the app shuts down tomorrow, do you lose everything again?
  • Maintenance reminders. The core value of a home maintenance app is telling you when things need attention before they break.
  • Photo documentation. Being able to photograph model plates, serial numbers, and receipts is essential for insurance claims and service calls.
  • Pricing model. Subscriptions add up. A $60/year app costs $300 over five years for what is ultimately a simple tracking tool.
  • Ease of input. If it's annoying to log maintenance, you won't do it. Look for quick-entry options like templates or voice input.

The 6 Best Centriq Alternatives

1. HomeGuard

$4.99 one time (14-day free trial)

HomeGuard was built specifically in response to the Centriq shutdown. Its defining feature is that it's entirely local-first: all data lives on your phone in an encrypted SQLite database. There are no servers, no accounts, and no cloud component that can be shut down. You can export your full history as JSON at any time.

It's also voice-first — you can tap the microphone and say "changed the furnace filter, remind me in 6 months" instead of navigating through menus. It ships with 65+ maintenance templates covering common items like HVAC systems, water heaters, and appliances, so setup takes a few minutes rather than hours.

Strengths
  • Can't be shut down (no cloud)
  • Voice commands for hands-free logging
  • $4.99 lifetime, no subscription
  • 65+ maintenance templates
  • Multi-property support
  • Full data export
Weaknesses
  • iOS only (no Android or web)
  • No cloud sync between devices
  • New app, smaller community
  • No manual/document scanning (yet)

2. Dib

~$120/year

Dib is the most feature-rich option, built by a former Slack engineer who has explicitly positioned it as a Centriq successor. Its standout feature is AI-powered automation: you can upload a product manual and Dib will automatically generate maintenance reminders from it. It also offers a home inventory feature and service provider recommendations.

Strengths
  • AI reads manuals and creates reminders
  • Comprehensive home inventory
  • Actively developed, strong roadmap
  • Web and mobile access
Weaknesses
  • Expensive (~$120/year)
  • Cloud-dependent (same risk as Centriq)
  • Subscription required for core features

3. HomeZada

$59–$99/year

HomeZada has been around for years and is the most established player in the space. It goes beyond basic maintenance tracking into home finance territory: property valuation estimates, remodeling cost calculators, and budgeting tools. If you own multiple properties and want financial tracking alongside maintenance, HomeZada is worth a look.

Strengths
  • Financial tools (valuation, budgets)
  • Multi-home support
  • Established company (10+ years)
  • Web, iOS, and Android
Weaknesses
  • $59–$99/year subscription
  • Dated interface
  • Overwhelming for basic maintenance needs
  • Cloud-dependent

4. Homer

$24.99/year

Homer won Product Hunt's Product of the Day in 2020 and has a loyal following. It focuses on clean design and a straightforward approach to tracking home items and their maintenance schedules. It includes an AI assistant for answering home-related questions and supports document downloads for products.

Strengths
  • Clean, well-designed interface
  • Reasonable price for a subscription
  • AI assistant for home questions
  • Product Hunt pedigree
Weaknesses
  • Annual subscription required
  • Cloud-dependent
  • Slower feature development recently

5. Dwellin

Free / Paid tiers

Dwellin differentiates itself with sustainability-focused features: energy efficiency insights, eco-friendly maintenance suggestions, and budgeting tools. It positions itself as the "first sustainable home management app." The free tier covers basic maintenance tracking, with paid tiers adding more advanced features.

Strengths
  • Free tier available
  • Eco-friendly insights
  • Budgeting and cost tracking
  • Active development
Weaknesses
  • Sustainability focus may not matter to everyone
  • Paid tier needed for full features
  • Cloud-dependent
  • Newer entrant, less proven

6. HomeLedger

Free / Paid tiers

HomeLedger takes a different angle: it's designed not just for homeowners but also for real estate agents and home builders who want to provide maintenance guidance to their clients. The "KeyCrew" feature connects homeowners with vetted service providers. If you want a direct line to contractors, HomeLedger is interesting.

Strengths
  • Free tier available
  • Service provider directory
  • Good for new homeowners (agent handoff)
  • Record keeping and reminders
Weaknesses
  • Business model tied to service providers
  • Cloud-dependent
  • Less focused on DIY homeowners
  • Feature set thinner for pure maintenance tracking

Which One Should You Pick?

It depends on what you need:

  • If data ownership is your top priority — and the Centriq shutdown is exactly why you're reading this — HomeGuard is the only app on this list where your data physically cannot be taken away by a company shutdown. Everything lives on your device, encrypted, and you can export it anytime.
  • If you want AI-powered automation and don't mind paying for it, Dib is impressive. Upload a manual and it generates your maintenance schedule automatically. At ~$120/year it's the most expensive option, but the time savings could justify it.
  • If you need financial tracking alongside maintenance — property valuations, remodel budgets, multi-home finances — HomeZada has the deepest feature set for that use case.
  • If you want something simple and well-designed at a moderate price, Homer at $24.99/year is a solid middle ground.
  • If you're on a tight budget, both Dwellin and HomeLedger offer free tiers that cover basic maintenance tracking.

Whichever app you choose, learn from the Centriq situation: make sure you can export your data. If an app doesn't offer a full data export, ask yourself what happens if that company shuts down in two years. Your home's maintenance history should outlast any single app.

Honorable mention: Upkept by Consumer Reports is a free tool that creates custom maintenance plans with step-by-step guidance. It's less of a full tracking app and more of a DIY reference tool, but it's excellent for learning what maintenance your home actually needs.
